Platform Overview

Molecular Exploration, Organized Around Your Research

Managing high-volume heterogeneous molecular repositories remains one of the principal challenges in modern computer-aided drug design (CADD) workflows. Public biological repositories often contain inconsistent metadata, incomplete experimental information, duplicate records, and records with varying levels of quality.

BioMolExplorer was designed to address these limitations through a flexible information retrieval architecture that integrates and standardizes datasets from PDB, ChEMBL, PubChem, and ZINC. The platform enables the automated generation of research-ready molecular datasets suitable for computational pharmacology, high-throughput virtual screening, network analysis, and comprehensive molecular similarity investigations.

The Flet workspace lets researchers create private projects, invite collaborators and connect scientific stages in the order required by their study. Separate ChEMBL and PubChem blocks retrieve compounds; PubChem also accepts a SMILES, CID or name directly and supports similarity searches. ADMET screening, graph selection, preparation, redocking and docking can be configured independently, with results and logs kept for each run. Scientific interpretations still require validation with study data.

Core Capabilities

Scientific Operations for Configurable Research Pipelines

BioMolExplorer combines automated retrieval pipelines, molecular fingerprinting, graph analysis, and redocking validation into a unified computational ecosystem.

Comprehensive Data Retrieval

Automated extraction pipelines for PDB, ChEMBL, PubChem, and ZINC repositories supporting computational screening initiatives.

Custom Filtering Framework

Configurable filters for EC number, bioactivity thresholds, molecule type, similarity score, molecular weight, experimental method, and structural quality.

Fingerprint Generation

Choose Morgan, MACCS, or pharmacophore per block. Similarity stages identify the algorithm from generated inputs automatically.

Similarity Modeling

Tanimoto-based molecular similarity analysis for clustering, scaffold identification, and candidate prioritization.

Automated Redocking

Validate receptor–ligand pairs with Vina, inspect RMSD and reference overlays, and reuse the prepared receptor in candidate docking.

Network Intelligence

Graph-centric analysis for detecting molecular communities, connected components, and common scaffolds.

Consensus Docking

Independent AutoDock Vina and DOCK6 docking from retrieved or imported compounds, with pose exchange in both directions. Consensus ranks common compound/receptor pairs with sortable normalized scores.

ADMET Profiling

Editable oral, CNS, peripheral, fragment and natural-product hypotheses. Local RDKit descriptors, structural alerts, optional ADMET-AI predictions and auditable screening subsets.

Flet Workspace

Your project. Your pipeline. Your collaborators.

Use collapsible navigation and execution groups in the sidebar, with contextual help for each action. Drag stages onto a spacious, zoomable canvas, organize their layout, connect their ports, and double-click each block to configure it. Guided forms handle filters and engine settings; advanced users can edit JSON and scientific templates. Start with a ready-made pipeline or bring your own data wherever the backend format allows.

Projects that travel with you

Choose a folder for each project. Export and import its configuration, inputs, results and versions to continue on another computer while reusing compatible completed experiments.

Collaborate with a shared history

Invite editors or viewers from the project's Share button. Autosaved edits appear for collaborators, while an attributed change history lets owners restore configuration, files and results to an earlier state.

Select the inputs your study needs

Combine selected output files and validated uploads in each block. Saved file selections are respected without repeating the selection dialog. Explore paginated compound tables directly in your results, or filter interactive ADMET EGG plots by BBB and HIA classification. Click a point to open its molecular 2D structure and download the selected dataset or chart.

Compound retrieval → ADMET
Fingerprints → Similarity → Graph selection → ADMET
Your prepared receptors + compounds → Vina → DOCK6 → Consensus

Prepared receptors must include the metadata and files expected by the docking protocol. See the input format guide.

Keep large experiments moving

Prepared receptors are reused; Vina affinity maps are shared within compatible batches, and DOCK6 grids within each receptor/site. Scientific commands have no implicit duration limit. Compounds that fail preparation or individual DOCK6 calculations are listed separately while the remaining candidates continue.

Completed runs bring stage summaries, tables, visual results and logs together. Remove obsolete stage results with confirmation and restore files through project history.

Workflow Architecture

Connect the Stages Your Study Needs

BioMolExplorer System Architecture

The scientific modules remain organized into retrieval and analysis workflows. Above them, the Flet workspace manages accounts, project permissions, files and pipeline dependencies. Each scientific stage runs in a separate Python process, with its own configuration snapshot, outputs and logs. The diagram below the heading illustrates the scientific workflow; the architecture guide documents the current application services and their limits.

Retrieval Stage

workflow/1-InformationRetrieval

Responsible for data extraction, normalization, and integration across molecular, structural, and bioactivity repositories.

pdb.py

PDB Integration

Downloads protein structures using EC number, resolution threshold, ligand presence, polymer entity, and experimental method filters.

retrieve_compounds.py

Compound Retrieval

Retrieves bioactivities, therapeutic targets, molecular entities, and expands the compound set with PubChem structural similars.

zinc.py

ZINC Streaming

Handles large-scale URI-based molecular library acquisition workflows for virtual screening applications.

Analysis Stage

workflow/2-Analysis

Responsible for fingerprint generation, similarity modeling, graph analysis, and automated redocking validation.

docking.py and redocking.py

Docking Analysis

Executes automated docking, redocking pipelines, and RMSD-based validation procedures for structural curation.

admet.py

ADMET profiling

Molecular property calculations, heuristic ADMET classifications, structural alerts, optional trained-model outputs and exported screening subsets.

dataAnalysis.py

Network Analytics

Generates molecular fingerprints, computes similarity matrices, and identifies connected molecular components.

Getting Started

Run the workspace in your scientific environment

Download the GitHub project using Git or Code → Download ZIP, then open a terminal in the source code folder. The Python package requires Python 3.12 or newer.

Before starting: install UCSF Chimera 1.17 and DOCK6 6.11.

These tools must be installed and configured on the computer running calculations. Installing the Python package or Conda environment does not install them. The interface may open without them, but dependent preparation, redocking and docking stages will fail.

Installation and executable checks (English) · Instalação e verificação (Português)

Install and start Flet

git clone https://github.com/mpiress/BioMolExplorer.git
cd BioMolExplorer
conda env create -f requirements.yml
conda activate BioMolExplorer
python -m pip install -e '.[ui]'
biomolexplorer-ui --web --dock6-path /path/to/dock6-6.11

Open http://127.0.0.1:8550 and create your account. If the environment already exists, activate it and install the interface.

Choose your execution environment

biomolexplorer-ui --web --no-browser \
  --data-dir ./workspace-data \
  --worker-python /path/to/python \
  --dock6-path /path/to/dock6-6.11

Check Chimera 1.17, DOCK6 6.11 and the DOCK6 accessory programs on PATH before starting. Molecular surfaces are generated by the native Python SES implementation; a standalone DMS executable is not required. Open Babel and Vina are also required by their protocols. Pass the complete DOCK6 installation root, containing bin/ and parameters/, to --dock6-path.

Use the workspace guide for accounts and pipelines, or the backend guide for CLI usage and parameters. The current executor uses a local Linux backend, SQLite and a bounded queue; see deployment and scientific limitations.

Molecular Analysis

Graph-Centric Molecular Similarity Intelligence

Connect one or more similarity stages, or upload validated external similarity CSVs. Each source produces a separate graph analysis, with isolated compounds preserved and the largest connected component (MCC) highlighted.

Explore full networks and their MCC with degree color scales, pan and zoom, and circular layouts. Hover to identify compounds and click to inspect molecular structures. MCC views show the common molecular fragment and offer downloadable figures combining its image with the network and degree statistics. Read the graph explorer guide.

Molecular inspection

Explore the pose in its receptor, relation by relation

Open Vina or DOCK6 results in the local WebGL viewer. Compare the best pose with its docking receptor and an available reference in their original coordinates. Redocking adds crystallographic ligand and complex layers; consensus offers separate 3D views for each engine.

Choose how to inspect each molecule

  • Set receptor and ligand styles independently: ribbons, sticks, spheres or lines as appropriate.
  • Show or hide ligand hydrogens retained in the file. Missing hydrogen atoms are not generated.
  • Toggle pose, receptor and reference layers; filter receptor chains and inspect atom details.
  • Show or hide interacting residue names independently of the dashed traces. Rotate, pan and zoom, then export the current scene as PNG.

Follow the dashed interaction traces

Hover over a trace for its type, residue, chain and distance. Matching legend colors and type filters help identify each relation; coincident traces are separated for inspection.

  • Green · Hydrogen bond
  • Pink · Parallel π–π
  • Coral · T-shaped π–π
  • Lilac · Hydrophobic contact
  • Lime green · Geometric van der Waals contact

Classification requires valid chemistry and residue identities; hydrogen bonds require explicit H. When classification is unavailable, heavy-atom distance contacts remain accessible. These 3D relations describe geometry. DOCK6 Footprint separately compares reference/final-pose energies per residue; neither display establishes experimental binding.
